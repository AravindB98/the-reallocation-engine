# Wage-level floor — Boston-Cambridge-Newton, MA-NH, 15-1252

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A check of the engineering job postings on the public job boards of 21 Massachusetts companies that have sponsored H-1B visas for software titles (7 had a matching posting in the person's area). Each range is placed against the Department of Labor's four official wage levels for the person's occupation and city. Since early 2026 those levels decide how many times an H-1B registration is entered in the lottery: Level I once, Level IV four times.

**Why read it.** In the Boston area a software developer needs at least $114,275 to reach Level I, $138,237 for Level II, $162,198 for Level III and $186,160 for Level IV. A posting's range tells you, before you apply, whether an offer from it can carry one lottery entry or two — and exactly how many dollars separate the two. No job board shows this number.

**What it found.** 13 matching postings in the person's area; 11 could be placed on a level, 2 could not (reasons below — the tool refuses rather than guesses). Of those placed: 5 start below Level I; 6 cross a level line inside their range. 11 of 11 change level if the employer files a different occupation code, so the code is a question to ask the recruiter. The next registration window falls inside the person's work authorization.

**What it does not decide.** It does not say whether to apply, whether the company will sponsor, or what you will be offered. The posted range is not the offer, the occupation code is the employer's choice, and the selection chances are the government's pre-rule projection, not measured results.

## What a person still has to judge

- **Board identity** — every board was found by guessing a URL from the company's website; none has been confirmed to belong to the company.
- **Pay evidence** — 6 placed postings rely on a dollar range read out of the description text, not the ATS pay field. Open each and confirm it is base pay for this worksite.
- **Occupation code** — the employer picks it, and it must match the job's duties. Ask which code the role is filed under; the last column shows when the answer changes your level. A lower-paid code can *raise* the level of the same salary — that is not a lever to ask for; filing a code that does not fit the duties is the employer's legal risk and a reason a petition is questioned.
- **Posting liveness** — a listing in the board feed is not proof the posting takes applications. Run the liveness check on any posting before you tailor an application.
- **The offer** — the registered level comes from the wage on the offer, not the posted range. Re-run with the offer once you have one.
- **Two registrations** — if two employers register you in the same season, the government uses the lower of the two levels.
