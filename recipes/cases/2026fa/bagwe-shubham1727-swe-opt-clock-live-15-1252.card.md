# swe-opt-clock-live — human card

## Executive summary

**What this is.** A one-page guide to a daily routine. It reads the real job boards of Boston-area companies that have sponsored software engineers, opens each new-grad posting to check that it's still open, and sorts the postings into apply now, network first, or drop. It works from public records and your own dates, not a chatbot's guess.

**Why read it.** On OPT, the deadline that bites first is usually your **90 days of unemployment**, not your OPT end date. Those days keep counting while you search. A posting can be open, at a company that sponsors, and a perfect fit, and still be impossible because hiring would finish after your days run out. This routine does that arithmetic for every posting, every morning, before you get attached to one.

**What it decides.** A recommendation per posting, and a plain list of what it couldn't check. You make the decision and sign it. You are asked to rate your fit only for postings that pass every other check, so your judgment goes where it can change something.

## About this card

**Audience:** a new-grad software engineer on F-1 OPT, currently unemployed, deciding which of today's real postings get today's hours.
**Agent twin:** `recipes/cases/2026fa/bagwe-shubham1727-swe-opt-clock-live-15-1252.md`
**Chapters:** 7 (who sponsors), 8 (is the job real), 10 (the visa clock), 11 (the scorer that combines them).

## What you do each morning (about 15 minutes)

1. Run the capture. It reads the boards and opens every new-grad software posting.
2. Read the capture audit. If a board says *proposed*, open it and decide whether it really is the company in the sponsorship record. Two different companies can share a name: on the first run, a board named "cohere" belonged to an AI company, not Cohere Health. Add your decision, with your name and the date, to the end of the decisions file. Never rewrite an old entry. Look before you confirm: the routine can't tell when you get this wrong.
3. Run the evaluation, then read the report top-down.
4. If there's a fit worksheet, rate those few postings (0 to 1, one line on why) and run it again.
5. Sign the decision sheet. Nothing is decided until you do.

## What it can check for you

- What each company's public board shows today, and that its filters agree with the engine's own board scan, count for count.
- Whether the posting page still opens to that posting with an apply button, and when it was checked.
- The company's H-1B approvals, denials, approval rate and sponsored job titles, and whether any sponsored software title is junior enough to count for a new grad.
- Your deadline: unemployment days used (projected to today), the date you must start by, and the earlier date your buffer asks for.
- A start date printed in a posting title. "June 2027 New Grads" means next year's class, which is too late for you.

## What it can't check (read this before trusting any line)

- **Whether a company will sponsor you, now.** The records show past approvals, with no year and no job code attached.
- **How long this company takes to hire.** Unless the title says, it assumes about 45 days. That's a guess, and the report labels it as one.
- **Postings on boards it doesn't read**, such as Workday or company-built sites.
- **Pages that hide the apply form.** Some career sites embed the application in a frame the checker can't see into. Those postings are held for you to open, not marked closed.
- **E-Verify, local pay, recent funding.** None of these is in the data yet.
- **Anything legal.** What counts as employment, and whether you can extend to STEM OPT, is your DSO's call.

## When it refuses

- A posting check older than 3 days: it asks you to capture again.
- Filters that disagree with the engine's scan, or a board nobody has confirmed: those postings aren't scored.
- Your dates missing or undated, or your unemployment days already used up: it stops and sends you to your DSO.
- Requests to score a held posting anyway, or to tweak a rating until a favourite passes: it refuses.

## What the result means for your day

| Result | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Apply, and you rated your fit at least 0.5 | your 2-hour research-and-apply block |
| Apply only because the company sponsors a lot (your fit under 0.5) | networking first. A strong sponsorship record alone is enough for the scorer to say Apply, even for a job asking for five years' experience |
| Consider, or a closed posting at a strong sponsor | your 3-hour networking block: talk to someone there first |
| Skip because of the clock | drop it now. Revisit only if a recruiter commits in writing to an early start |
| Held | you, now, for five minutes: open the board or the posting and record what you saw |
