# What the posting says about sponsorship — human card

**Audience:** an international master's student on F-1, before OPT, applying for network engineering
jobs, who has to decide which postings are worth an application.
**Agent twin:** `recipes/cases/2026fa/prpathak1-network-sponsorship-text.md` (status DRAFT)

## Executive summary

You have a short list of network engineering jobs. Before applying, you want to know one thing that
most job boards hide: **what does this posting itself say about visa sponsorship or work
authorization?** This workflow pulls the exact sentences out of each live posting and shows them to
you, with a label you approve:

- **supported**: it says it offers sponsorship;
- **contradicted**: it rules you out. "Inferred" means the rule-out comes from a federal rule about
  security clearances, not from the posting's own words;
- **insufficient**: it doesn't say. Silence is not a "no".

A company's past sponsorship record is shown beside the posting, never as the answer for that posting.

On its first run (2026-10-20), none of 7 in-scope postings offered sponsorship. 2 required a security
clearance, and 5 said nothing.

## What it can verify

- The sentence is in the posting, word for word, on the date it was fetched, and the section it sits
  in (Required vs Preferred).
- The posting was live when checked.
- The company's stored H-1B approval counts (80 Days to Stay).

## What it cannot verify

- What the employer would actually do if you asked.
- Whether a clearance exception (a "Limited Access Authorization") would be granted. The federal rule
  allows one "in those rare circumstances".
- Whether a "Software Engineer, Network…" job is filed under your occupation code.
- Postings on boards it cannot read.

## What you do

1. Read each extracted sentence and its section.
2. Approve or change the proposed label, and write your reason.
3. Decide whether each borderline title is your occupation.
4. Spend application effort on what's left. Treat "insufficient" as "ask a recruiter", not as "no".

## When to stop

If a posting has no approved label, or its link is no longer live, it is not on your list.
